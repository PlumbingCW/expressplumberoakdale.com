What to do when the tank fails

Replace when the tank body leaks, when the unit is well past a dozen years or when repair would cost close to half the price of a new heater. A tank that leaks cannot be sealed from the inside, because water is escaping through the steel shell.

  • Water or rust streaks on the floor around the base
  • Rusty or discolored hot water coming from the taps only
  • Repeated repairs in a short span
  • A heater that cannot meet the household need any more

What we do on replacement day

  1. Shut off and drain

    We turn off the gas or power and water, then drain the old tank safely.

  2. Remove and haul

    The old unit goes out and gets taken with us.

  3. Set and connect

    The new heater is set on a pan or stand, and the water, gas or electric lines are connected with new flex connectors.

  4. Safety fittings

    The relief valve, discharge pipe, expansion tank where needed and seismic strapping are installed.

  5. Test and walk through

    We light or power the unit, check for leaks and show you the shutoff and the thermostat.

The price is set before the old tank is touched. If the existing venting or gas line needs work, we point it out first and explain why.

Choosing a size and fuel type

A heater that is too small runs out in the middle of a shower, and one that is too large burns fuel keeping extra water hot. We size by the number of people, the number of bathrooms and whether a tub or a large soaking tub is used often.

Gas units recover faster and cost less to run where the line already exists. Electric units are simpler to vent and suit places without a gas line. If space is tight or you want endless hot water, a tankless water heater is worth a look, though the installation takes more planning.

Permits, code and the details people forget

California requires water heaters to be braced against earthquake movement, and many installations also need a permit. The City of Oakdale Building Division handles permits at (209) 845-3625, and we recommend confirming the current requirement with them for your address.

Other details matter too. The discharge line from the relief valve should run to a safe place, the heater should sit on a base that suits the location, and combustion air must be available for gas units. We handle those items so they do not turn into problems at resale.

Code items included in a replacement

A replacement follows the California Plumbing Code, which rests on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That means a relief valve and discharge line, a thermal expansion tank where the system is closed, and seismic bracing under California Health and Safety Code section 19211.

What happens after the new heater is in

Set the thermostat to a safe temperature, since very hot water is a scald risk for children and older adults. Plan a yearly flush to clear sediment, which matters with the moderately hard water the city wells supply.

If an older heater failed because of leaks upstream, ask about repairs to supply lines at the same visit. We cover Oakdale and nearby Escalon with the same replacement service.
