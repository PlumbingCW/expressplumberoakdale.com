Slab Leak Signs in Oakdale, CA

The common signs of a slab leak are a warm spot on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off, a meter that keeps turning and a bill that climbs for no reason. Any of these is a reason to test the lines.

This guide lists the signs, the checks you can do at home and how a plumber confirms the leak without cutting the floor first.

  • Published November 16, 2026
  • 6 min read

The warning signs

  • Warm or hot patches on the floor, often with no heater nearby
  • Water running sounds in a quiet house
  • The meter moving with every tap off
  • Damp carpet or lifting flooring
  • Cracks in tile or grout lines that follow a pipe
  • Low water pressure or a rise in your water bill
  • Mildew smells near baseboards

One sign can have an easy explanation. Several together deserve a pressure test.

Checks you can do at home

  1. Do the meter test

    Turn off all water and watch the meter for movement.

  2. Close the main

    If the meter stops, the leak is inside. If not, it is outside.

  3. Walk the floor barefoot

    Feel for warm spots, especially along hot water lines.

  4. Listen at quiet times

    At night, put your ear near the floor and the walls.

If the water heater runs more than usual or the hot water is slow to arrive, a hot line may be leaking under the slab.

Slab homes in the Oakdale area

Many homes in newer parts of the valley are built on concrete slabs, with copper or plastic lines laid in or under the floor. The older core near the original townsite more often has raised foundations and crawlspaces, where leaks can be reached from below.

Valley soil and the moderately hard water the city supplies can wear on copper over decades. The city report we reviewed listed total hardness near 88 mg/L, and water chemistry is one of many factors in how long pipe lasts. A pinhole leak can start from the outside, where concrete and soil act on the pipe.

How a plumber confirms it

We pressure test each line to learn which one is leaking, then use an acoustic detector and a thermal camera to find the spot. A moisture meter and a line tracer confirm the path.

The result is a location within a few inches, which means a smaller opening. For repair choices, see slab leak repair, and for other hidden leaks, leak detection explains the tools.

What happens if you wait

A slab leak does not heal. Water softens soil under the slab and can cause settling and cracks. It also feeds mold in the subfloor and under the flooring, and the water bill climbs the whole time.

Waiting also raises repair cost, since damage spreads. Call as soon as you suspect one. If water is already surfacing, close the main shutoff and call for emergency help.

Hot lines versus cold lines

Hot water lines wear faster because heat expands and contracts the pipe over and over. A slab leak on a hot line often shows as a warm patch and an unusually busy water heater.

Cold line leaks are quieter and may show only as damp flooring and a meter that moves. A pressure test on each line tells us which one is failing, and that decides the repair route.

Insurance and documentation

Many policies cover the water damage from a sudden leak but not the pipe itself, and they often exclude damage from slow leaks that went unnoticed. Read your policy and call your insurer early.

Keep the plumber report, photos of the damage and your meter readings. Clear records help a claim go smoothly, and we provide a written report of what we found at the end of every locate.

Quick notes on cost and timing

A locate is a small visit, and the repair price depends on the route chosen. A spot repair opens the floor in one place, while a reroute through the attic or wall avoids the slab altogether.

We explain both options and the trade offs, so the choice is yours. Most homeowners are surprised at how fast a locate goes once the leaking line is isolated.

Choosing who finds it

Pick a plumber who locates the leak before cutting. Ask what tools they use, whether they will show you the reading and whether the quote covers floor patching. A contractor who wants to start cutting without testing is guessing with your floor.

Check the license on the Contractors State License Board website before you hire. We locate first, show you the result and give a price before the work begins.

Preventing slab leaks

You cannot prevent every leak, but you can lower the risk. Keep water pressure within a safe range, replace worn pressure regulators and avoid chemical drain cleaners that stress pipe.

If your home has a history of pinhole leaks, ask about repiping before the next one. We serve Riverbank and Waterford as well as Oakdale.

Testing standards for a suspected leak

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) relies on pressure testing to prove water piping is sound. A plumber uses the same approach to confirm a slab leak before any cutting begins.
