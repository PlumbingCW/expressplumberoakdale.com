Is it really a leak? Check the meter

Turn off every tap, the ice maker and the irrigation. Open the meter box near the street and look at the small triangle or wheel on the dial. If it moves while nothing in the house is running, water is going somewhere.

Most Oakdale meters sit in a box at the curb or the edge of the lot. A meter that stops when you close the main shutoff valve at the house tells you the leak is inside, while one that keeps moving points to the service line between the meter and the house. That single test splits the search in half, and it costs nothing to try.

How we locate a leak without tearing things open

We start with a pressure test to confirm which line is losing water, then narrow the area with listening gear. An acoustic detector picks up the hiss of pressurized water in a pipe, and a handheld thermal camera shows the cool or warm patch a hot or cold line leaves behind.

Moisture meters confirm what the tools suggest, and the final opening is usually a small hole in drywall or a short cut in a floor. The goal is to open one spot, fix it and patch it, not to chase water through the whole house.

Where leaks hide in valley homes

Meter and high bill

A running toilet flapper, a dripping hose bib or a service line leak can raise the bill with no visible wet spot.

Ceilings and walls

Supply lines and drain joints above a ceiling stain it slowly. Brown rings that grow after a shower point to a drain, not the supply.

Crawlspaces

Older raised foundations hide corroded lines and failing fittings. Damp soil under one bathroom is a clue.

Irrigation and yard

A soggy patch or a lush stripe of grass in dry weather often marks a buried line break.

Pool and spa lines

Equipment plumbing near the pad can leak at unions and valves and mimic evaporation loss.

Under the slab

Copper in a concrete floor can pinhole from the outside in. See slab leak repair for the fix.

Why leaks matter more than the water bill

Hidden water feeds mold, softens subfloors and stains finishes long before the bill says anything. Valley summers are dry, which hides the signs, and a slow leak under a floor can run for months.

Catching it early keeps the repair small. A one fitting fix in a wall is far cheaper than replacing drywall, flooring and insulation after the damage spreads. If a burst pipe has already flooded a room, shut off the main first and call for emergency help.

Testing that code already requires

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which the California Plumbing Code is built on, requires new and altered water piping to be pressure tested before it is covered. Leak detection uses the same idea on an existing system, by isolating a line and watching whether it holds pressure.
