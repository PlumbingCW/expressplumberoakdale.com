Emergency Faucet Repair in Oakdale, CA

A steady drip wastes water, a stiff handle gets worse and a leak under the sink can damage the cabinet. Most faucet trouble comes from a worn part that can be replaced without replacing the whole fixture.

We repair kitchen, bathroom and outdoor faucets, and we replace the supply lines and shutoff valves that sit under them.

What to do first when a faucet or line leaks

Every sink should have two small shutoff valves, one for hot and one for cold. Turn them clockwise to stop the water to that faucet while you wait. If they will not turn or they drip, they need replacement.

Older valves often seize because they have not moved in years. Forcing them can snap a stem, so stop if they resist and call. We carry replacements and fit them at the same visit.

Moderately hard water leaves mineral buildup in faucets over time, which is why cartridges wear out. The city water report we reviewed listed total hardness near 88 mg/L.

Repair or replace the faucet

A faucet in good shape with a worn part is worth repairing. A corroded body, a finish that has peeled or parts that are no longer made points toward a new faucet.

We can supply a new faucet or install one you bought. We check the shutoff valves and the supply lines at the same time, because swapping a faucet on tired valves creates another leak risk.

Outdoor faucets and freeze risk

Hose bibs can freeze in the cold snaps the valley sees each winter. A frost proof bib has a long stem that shuts off inside the heated wall, and an old style bib does not.

Disconnect hoses before cold nights and cover the bib. If a bib dripped all summer, replacing it before winter is much cheaper than repairing a split pipe later. For a burst, see burst pipe repair.

Kitchen faucet details worth knowing

Pull down sprayers and touchless models have hoses and solenoids that wear. A sprayer that leaks under the sink or a handle that lags often needs a hose or a cartridge, not a new faucet.

Bathroom faucets with pop up drains add another place to leak. The drain linkage behind the faucet can loosen and drip into the cabinet, and tightening or replacing it is a short job. When we repair one, we run water for a few minutes and check every joint with a dry paper towel so a slow drip does not hide.

When a faucet leak is really a plumbing leak

Water under the sink does not always come from the faucet. A loose drain, a cracked trap or a leaking disposal can drip into the same cabinet, and a bucket under the leak hides the real source.

We dry the cabinet, run each fixture one at a time and watch where the water first appears. That method finds the true source in minutes and avoids replacing a part that was never at fault.

Backflow and flow rate rules for faucets

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), protects drinking water from backflow, which is why spouts must sit above the flood level of a sink. State efficiency rules also cap the flow of new faucets, and we check both when swapping one.
