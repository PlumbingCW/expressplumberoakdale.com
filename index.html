Plumbing in a valley town with a long history

Oakdale began as a rail meeting point in 1871 and was incorporated in 1906. Many homes near the original townsite along F Street are decades old, and older homes tend to have clay or cast iron drains, mixed supply pipe and fixtures that have never been replaced. That is why a camera look and a pipe check are part of how we diagnose.

The City of Oakdale supplies water from groundwater wells. The city report we reviewed listed total hardness near 88 mg/L, which is moderately hard, so scale in water heaters and faucets is a steady, slow problem. All our work follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). That report is from 2013, and the city can send you its current one.

Summers are hot and dry, with historic July highs averaging in the low 100s, and a few winter nights drop to freezing. Both extremes show up in plumbing, as irrigation leaks and busy water heaters in summer, and split exposed pipes after a hard freeze.

Open 24 hours, every day

Plumbing problems do not wait for business hours. A supply line that fails at midnight does as much damage as one that fails at noon, so the emergency line is staffed all night, on weekends and on holidays.

When you call, we ask a few questions, such as where the water is, whether you can reach the main shutoff and which drains are affected. Those answers help us send the right crew and the right equipment, and they often let us give you steps that limit the damage before we arrive.
