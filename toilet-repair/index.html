Emergency Toilet Repair in Oakdale, CA

A toilet that runs all night, rocks on the floor, leaks at the base or will not flush is more than an annoyance. A running toilet wastes water every hour, and a leaking one can ruin a floor.

We repair fill valves, flappers, wax rings and flanges, and we replace toilets when repair no longer makes sense.

  • Wax ring and flange repair
  • Replacement when needed

If the toilet is overflowing now

  1. Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down to stop the flow, or close the small valve on the wall behind the toilet.
  2. Turn the valve clockwise until it stops.
  3. Put towels around the base and keep people away from the water.
  4. Do not flush again until a plumber has checked the line.

If other drains are also slow, the blockage may be in the main line. Mention that when you call.

Flush mechanics are the most common culprit and cost the least to fix.

Wax rings and flanges

The seal under the toilet is a wax ring that sits between the toilet horn and the floor flange. When it fails, water seeps onto the subfloor, often with no visible puddle for months.

A soft spot near the toilet, a faint sewer smell or a toilet that moves are warning signs. We pull the toilet, inspect the flange and subfloor and reset with a new seal. If the flange is broken, we repair it before the toilet goes back, since a new ring on a cracked flange fails again.

Repair or replace

An older toilet with a hairline crack in the bowl or tank cannot be saved. A toilet that clogs constantly may also be a poor design. Replacement can be quick, and newer models flush well with less water.

If you are remodeling, bring us in early. Our plumbing installation work covers new fixtures and the rough in behind them.

Low flow and water saving

Many older toilets use far more water per flush than current models. A leaking flapper can waste more than a few gallons a day without a sound, which shows up on the bill as a number that creeps upward.

If your toilet is two decades old and clogs often, replacement may save water and frustration. We can recommend models with a strong flush and a wide trapway, set them on a fresh seal and haul the old unit away.

Water efficiency rules for toilets

California limits new toilets to 1.28 gallons per flush, and the California Plumbing Code, built on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets how a toilet must be anchored to its flange. We follow both when we reset or replace a toilet.

Tips that prevent a repeat

Use only toilet paper, replace flappers every few years and check for a silent leak by adding a few drops of food coloring to the tank. If color appears in the bowl without flushing, the flapper is leaking.
