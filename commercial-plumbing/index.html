What to do when a business plumbing line fails

  • Close the affected restrooms or kitchen stations before water spreads.
  • Shut off the water to the fixture or the building if the leak is large.
  • Move stock, paper goods and electronics away from the wet area.
  • Call us and describe the problem, including whether more than one fixture is affected.

If sewage is rising from a floor drain, stop all water use. Health rules for food service can require closing until the line is clear and the area is cleaned.

Grease, traps and backflow

Restaurants and food shops depend on grease traps and interceptors that must be serviced on a schedule set by the local sewer authority. A full trap sends grease into the line and causes backups that are costly to clear.

Backflow prevention devices protect the public water supply and are tested on a schedule in many places. We can inspect the plumbing around these devices and tell you what we see, and we point you to the right certified tester or the water provider where a formal test is required.

Keeping disruption low

We plan around your hours when we can. A repair done before opening, or after closing, avoids losing a busy lunch or evening, and we bring the parts needed to finish in one trip when the problem allows.

We also explain what we found and what is optional, so you can decide what to do now and what to schedule. A clear report helps landlords and tenants sort out who handles what.

Maintenance that prevents emergencies

A schedule for drain jetting, heater flushing, valve checks and fixture inspection costs less than a surprise shutdown. We can set a plan that matches your use, such as a quarterly visit for a kitchen and a yearly one for an office.

Tell us about your building, your hours and your past problems, and we will suggest a plan that fits. Small checks, such as a leaking flush valve or a slow floor drain, often point to bigger trouble.

What we need to know when you call

Tell us what is not working, how many fixtures are affected and whether the problem started suddenly or built up. Mention the building type, any known shutoff location and whether the space is open to the public right now.

Those details let us bring the right machine and parts the first time. For tenants, send the landlord contact if approval is needed, since some repairs need an owner to sign off before work can start.

Commercial rules we plan around

Commercial plumbing follows the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which includes rules for grease interceptors and traps. Cross connection control for public water systems is set by California Code of Regulations Title 17, so backflow devices must be tested by a certified tester.
