About Burchell Hill

The Stanislaus Local Agency Formation Commission reviewed Oakdale's services in 2015 and listed Burchell Hill as one of the city's specific plans, along with Bridle Ridge. It states that both are largely built out and contain mostly residential uses.

Real estate listing sites, which we treat only as supporting evidence, show mostly single family homes of about 1,600 to 3,300 square feet with three to five bedrooms, built from about 2000 to 2002. They list streets such as Burchell Hill Drive, Irvin Court and Cindy Drive. The sources reviewed do not publish exact boundaries.

Homes that are about a quarter century old are past the point where original water heaters, valves and fixtures can be taken for granted, even though many have been replaced.

A boosted pressure zone and what it means at your tap

A boosted zone is one where pumps raise water pressure. The service review says Burchell Hill is served that way, but it does not say what pressure a particular house sees. High pressure can wear out fixtures early, so it is worth a minute to measure it.

A pressure gauge that screws onto an outdoor hose bib reads static pressure. Readings above about 80 psi point to a regulator that is missing or worn.

  • Pipes that hammer when a valve closes can mean high pressure. A plumber can also check the expansion tank on the water heater.
  • A toilet that runs or refills on its own may be pressure or a worn part, covered on the toilet repair page.
  • Dripping faucets that return after repair can point the same way. See faucet repair.
  • A water bill that rises with no change in use calls for leak detection.

Homes from the early 2000s

At this age the questions are about service life, not mystery. Tank water heaters often last around a decade, so many original units have already been swapped. Angle stops under sinks, supply hoses and shutoff valves are worth exercising once a year.

If the main shutoff has not been turned in years, test it before an emergency. A valve that will not close turns a small leak into a large one.

Before you call

  • Close the main shutoff if water is flowing.
  • If you own a pressure gauge, note the reading at a hose bib.
  • Note when the hammering, running or dripping happens.
  • Write down the age of the water heater from its label.

Those details help the plumber narrow the problem on the phone and explain the price.
