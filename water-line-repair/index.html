Emergency Water Line Repair in Oakdale, CA

Low pressure, a soggy patch in the yard or a meter that keeps turning with the house shut off often means the service line has failed. It runs from the meter to the house, and it is your responsibility to maintain.

We find the break, repair or replace the line and restore pressure with as little digging as the situation allows.

  • Trench or trenchless options

Signs of a failing service line

  • A wet, spongy or unusually green patch between the meter and the house
  • Pressure that drops through the day or the whole house
  • A meter dial that spins when the house main is closed
  • Discolored water after a pressure change
  • The sound of water running when nothing is on

Close the main at the house and check the meter. If it still runs, the break is on the street side of that valve.

Materials and why lines fail

Older lots may have galvanized steel, which rusts through, or early plastic lines that cracked with age. Copper holds up well but can pit in aggressive soil. Newer lines in the area are often polyethylene or copper.

Roots, shifting soil and heavy equipment crossing a yard can crush or pull a joint. Seasonal soil movement in the valley also works on old fittings, and a line that survived for decades can fail in one dry summer.

How we repair a service line

  1. Locate the break

    Pressure testing and listening tools find the leak so we dig in the right place.

  2. Call for utility marking

    We arrange line marking before any digging to protect gas, power and sewer.

  3. Open and repair

    A short trench exposes the pipe and the damaged part is cut out and replaced.

  4. Test and backfill

    The line is pressure tested before the trench is closed and the surface restored.

If the pipe is in poor shape along its length, replacing the run gives a lasting fix. We show you the difference in cost between a spot repair and full replacement before you decide.

Pressure and the pressure regulator

Many homes have a pressure regulator near the main shutoff. A failing regulator can cause high pressure that stresses pipes or low pressure that mimics a leak, so we test it when we work on the service line.

Replacing a worn regulator at the same time as the line repair is often sensible. It is a small part that protects the whole system, and the water is already shut off for the job.

Permits and the meter

The meter belongs to the utility, and work at or beyond it may involve the City of Oakdale. We handle the private side and coordinate on any connection that needs the city, so you are not caught between parties.

Ask the city if you are unsure who owns a section of the line. A short call before work starts avoids confusion later.

Pipe standards for a service line

Pipe and fittings that carry drinking water must be listed to NSF/ANSI 61 and approved under the California Plumbing Code, which follows the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). The same code sets the 80 psi limit that a pressure regulator near the main is there to protect.
