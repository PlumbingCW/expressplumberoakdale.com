Signs the sewer line is damaged

  • Several drains slow or back up together, especially the lowest fixtures
  • Sewage smell in the yard or near the cleanout
  • Green, lush or soggy patches in the lawn along the line
  • Gurgling in toilets when other fixtures drain
  • Repeated clogs that cables clear only for a short time

One slow drain is usually a local clog. When more than one fixture is affected, the main lateral becomes the suspect, and a camera tells us why.

What damages sewer lines in the valley

Older neighborhoods near the original townsite may still have clay or cast iron laterals, which crack, separate at joints and scale inside. Roots find the joints and swell inside the pipe over time.

Soil shifts can leave a section sagging into a belly where waste collects. Newer plastic lines can be crushed or offset by heavy equipment during construction or landscaping. A camera is the only way to tell these causes apart.

Digging or trenchless

Open trench repair exposes the pipe and lets us see the whole problem, which is why it is common. Trenchless methods such as pipe lining or bursting can reduce the digging when the line is suited to it, and we explain when it is a good fit.

Driveways, trees and fences affect the choice. A short dig to a cracked joint costs far less than a full replacement, and a good camera inspection avoids paying for work that is not needed.

Permits, inspections and the work area

Sewer work in California may require a permit and an inspection. Confirm the process for your property with the City of Oakdale, and we will coordinate with the inspector on the repair schedule.

We call before digging to mark utilities and protect landscaping where we can. When the trench is closed, we compact the soil and restore the surface as agreed, and we leave a cleanout so the next service is easy.

Code rules that apply to a sewer repair

A repaired lateral must follow the California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which sets approved pipe materials, joint methods and a minimum slope so waste flows. Work on the part of the line in a street or easement may also involve the City of Oakdale.
