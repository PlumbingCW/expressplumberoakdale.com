What to do while a drain is backing up

Stop sending water down the system. Every flush, load of laundry or dishwasher cycle adds to whatever is already stuck, and it can push the overflow into the lowest fixture in the house, usually a shower or a floor drain.

  • Turn off the washing machine and dishwasher until the drain is cleared.
  • Do not pour chemical drain openers into standing water, because the next person to work on the line can be burned by it.
  • Keep children and pets out of any water that came up from a floor drain or tub.
  • Note which fixtures are slow. One slow sink points to a branch line, while several fixtures at once point to the main.

Those notes save time on the phone. When the tub and the toilet back up together, the clog is usually past the last branch, and the plumber will plan for a main line cleaning from the start.

How we clear a drain

Most household clogs are grease, hair, soap film, wipes or paper that stalled at a bend. A hand cable or a drum machine with the right cutting head breaks them up and pulls them out, and the line is flushed afterward so loose debris does not settle one fitting downstream.

When a cable cannot tell us what is wrong, we run a camera. Seeing a cracked clay joint, a sagging section or a root mass changes the fix completely. Clearing the same root ball every few months is a bandage, while repairing the joint ends the call-backs. If the pipe wall is coated rather than blocked, hydro jetting scours the walls clean in a way a cable cannot.

Where Oakdale clogs usually start

Older homes near the F Street corridor and the original townsite often still have clay or cast iron drain lines, and those roughen with age until paper and grease catch on them. Mature trees along older streets add roots that find the joints, especially in the dry months when roots hunt for moisture.

Newer subdivisions on plastic pipe see a different mix. Kitchen grease, flushed wipes and long hair do most of the damage, and a lot of them clog at the first change of direction past the fixture. A clog that returns inside a few weeks is a sign of a partial obstruction or a pipe flaw, not bad luck.

Keeping drains running between visits

Collect cooking grease in a can instead of the sink, and use a strainer in the shower and kitchen. Only toilet paper belongs in the toilet, including products labeled flushable, since they break down far slower than paper.

A seasonal run of hot water down kitchen drains does little if grease has already set. If a branch is slow once a month, ask for a camera look rather than another cable run, and consider a cleanout if your main line has none. A plumber can also fit a new cleanout so the next clog is cheaper to reach.

What the plumbing code expects of a drain

The California Plumbing Code is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and requires every fixture drain to have a trap and a vent, with cleanout access at set points along the line. A drain with no cleanout is not only harder to clear. It can also be a code gap worth fixing during the visit.
