Tankless Water Heater Heating Element Problems in Oakdale, CA

If an electric tankless heater gives lukewarm water, trips a breaker or shows an error code, a failed or scaled heating element is a likely cause. Replacing the element is often cheaper than replacing the unit, as long as the rest of the heater is sound.

This guide explains the symptoms, what causes elements to fail and how to decide between a repair and a new heater.

Gas or electric first

Electric tankless heaters have heating elements, usually in a set of two to four. Gas tankless units use a burner and a heat exchanger, so the word element does not apply in the same way. Check the label to see which you have before you chase a part.

If your unit is gas, the troubleshooting below still helps, but the failing part is more likely the igniter, the flame sensor or a scaled heat exchanger, which we cover on our tankless water heater page.

An element is a resistive coil. A multimeter test tells you if it is open or shorted, and that is a job for someone comfortable around high voltage.

Why elements fail early

Scale is the main cause. Minerals cling to hot surfaces and form an insulating crust. The element runs hotter to push heat through the crust and wears out faster.

Water that sits in the unit between uses can also leave deposits. Low water flow and repeated short cycles increase the stress. Regular descaling keeps the elements clean and extends their life, and a softener or scale filter cuts the source.

Oakdale water and tankless service

The city water we reviewed listed total hardness near 88 mg/L, a moderately hard figure that builds scale slowly but steadily. In a tankless unit, which heats a small volume through narrow passages, even modest hardness shows up within a few years.

That report is from 2013, so use the city current report and ask us about descaling on a yearly or two year schedule. Homes served from other wells nearby can see higher or lower numbers, and Waterford reported soft water in its own data.

Repair or replace

If the unit is under about ten years old and only one or two elements have failed, replacement of the elements and a descaling is usually worth it. Parts are available for common models, and a repair costs less than a new heater.

If the unit is older, has repeated failures or has a cracked housing or heat exchanger, replacement is wiser. A planned install is covered on our water heater installation page, and an urgent failure is handled through water heater replacement.

Questions to ask when a unit is under warranty

Many tankless heaters carry a longer warranty on the heat exchanger than on the electronics and the elements. Read the terms, since scale damage and installation errors are common reasons a claim is denied.

Keep records of descaling and service, because a maker may ask for them. A plumber who documents the visit helps you make the claim, and a service valve kit makes descaling easy to do and to prove.

What a service visit includes

  1. Check the error history

    We read the codes and look for patterns, such as a heater that trips under load.

  2. Test the elements

    Resistance and continuity readings show which elements are weak.

  3. Descale the unit

    A pump circulates a mild solution through the unit to dissolve scale.

  4. Verify flow and temperature

    We run hot water at several fixtures and confirm steady output.

Choosing between element repair and a new heater

Add up the cost of parts and labor and compare it to a new unit, then weigh the age. A heater with two failed elements and a fresh warranty period is a repair. A unit with failing electronics, a corroded housing and many years of service is a replacement.

We give you both numbers when it is a close call, so you can choose with the cost in front of you. If you pick replacement, we haul the old unit away and set the new one to code.

Safety note

Never open an electric tankless heater with power on. Turn off the breaker and test for voltage first. The wiring and the controls hold enough power to injure, and a mistake can also void the warranty.

Electrical standards for electric units

Electric tankless heaters draw a large load and must be wired to the National Electrical Code (NEC) on a properly sized circuit. That is one reason element work is best left to someone comfortable with high power equipment.
