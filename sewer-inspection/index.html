Sewer Inspection in Oakdale, CA

A sewer camera shows what the pipe looks like on the inside: roots, cracks, offsets, bellies and buildup. It turns a guess into a recorded answer, which matters before you buy a house or pay for a dig.

We run the camera, show you the picture as we go and explain what the footage means.

Why inspect the sewer line

The lateral is buried, so problems stay hidden until they cause a backup. A camera finds them early, when a repair is smaller and cheaper, and it can confirm that a line is healthy when you are about to buy.

Repeat clogs are the other common reason. If a cable keeps clearing the same line, the camera shows the reason it fills again.

What the camera can find

Root intrusion

Roots entering joints and filling the pipe.

Cracks and breaks

Longitudinal cracks, broken sections and missing pipe.

Offset joints

Sections pushed out of line by soil movement.

Bellies

Sags where water and waste collect.

Scale and buildup

Mineral or grease deposits narrowing the pipe.

Wrong pipe or tie ins

Mismatched materials or poorly made connections.

Buying or selling a home

A general home inspection does not look inside the sewer line. For an older house, a camera inspection before closing can protect you from a large surprise, and the recorded footage can support a repair credit negotiation.

Sellers also benefit. Knowing the line condition before listing avoids a late discovery that stalls a sale. If the camera finds trouble, we can quote sewer line repair from the same footage.

How the inspection works

  1. Find access

    We use an existing cleanout or pull a toilet if none exists.

  2. Run the camera

    A flexible cable carries a small camera through the line while a monitor shows the view.

  3. Mark the location

    A locator tracks the camera so any problem can be mapped to a surface spot.

  4. Review the footage

    We go through the findings with you and recommend only what is needed.

Questions to settle before the visit

Tell us why you want the inspection so we can focus on it. Mention previous backups, the age of the house and any trees near the line, as these help us interpret the footage.

If the line is full of debris, we may need to clear it first for the camera to pass. In that case, drain cleaning or hydro jetting is quoted separately so you can decide.

Reading the footage like a pro

Ask the technician to pause on anything unclear. A shadow can be a stain, a root hair or a hairline crack, and the difference decides whether you need a repair or only cleaning. Clay lines show joints every few feet, which is normal, while a gap or a lip at a joint is not.

Notes matter as much as pictures. We tell you the distance from the cleanout to each finding and the pipe material, so another contractor can use the same report to bid the work. A good inspection leaves you able to compare options instead of depending on one opinion.

How often to inspect

A line in good shape does not need yearly checks. Older clay or cast iron lines, houses with large trees nearby and properties with a history of backups are worth a look every few years, and always before a purchase or a major landscaping project.

A camera report and the code

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) and the California Plumbing Code set a minimum slope and require sound joints in a building sewer. A recorded camera run is the practical way to check a buried line against those expectations without digging.
