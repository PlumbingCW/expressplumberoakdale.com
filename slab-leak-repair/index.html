Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Oakdale, CA

A warm patch on the floor, the sound of running water when everything is off or a water bill that keeps climbing can mean a pressurized pipe is leaking under the concrete. The sooner it is found, the smaller the repair.

We locate the leak without guessing and then repair, reroute or replace the line depending on what the pipe condition allows.

Signs of a slab leak

  • A warm area on a tile or hardwood floor where a hot line runs
  • Running water noise when every fixture is off
  • A meter that moves with the house shut down
  • Damp carpet, lifting laminate or cracks in the tile grout
  • Low water pressure with no clear cause
  • A musty smell with no visible source

Any one sign could have another explanation, which is why we test before we recommend a repair.

What causes leaks under concrete

Many valley homes built on slabs have copper lines running through or beneath the concrete. Corrosion from the outside, abrasion against gravel and slight foundation movement can wear a pinhole in a pipe over years.

Hot lines fail more often because heat expands the pipe repeatedly. Water chemistry, including moderate hardness, can contribute to pitting inside older copper. None of this is anyone's fault, but it is common enough that it deserves a careful look when the symptoms show up.

How we find the leak

We shut the supply down in sections and pressure test each line to learn which one is losing water. Then an acoustic detector and a thermal camera are used on the floor to pinpoint the spot within a few inches.

Finding it exactly matters. A precise locate means a small opening in the slab instead of a large one, and it protects the rest of the floor. Our leak detection page describes the same tools used for other hidden leaks.

Repair options

Reroute through walls or atticPipe is aging or access is difficultLeaves old pipe abandoned in the slab
Repipe the affected linesSeveral leaks or widespread pittingHigher cost, longest lasting fix

We explain which route fits the pipe condition and your budget. Rerouting often avoids breaking the floor and gives a clean result. For wide failure, see repiping.

Protecting the house during the work

We set up dust control and protect nearby surfaces when opening a slab. Concrete is cut with a wet saw, the pipe repaired and tested, and the slab patched to the level of the floor.

Floor finish matching is a separate matter. Tile, hardwood and carpet each have their own repair path, and we tell you what to expect before cutting so the repair does not leave a surprise.

Materials and testing after a slab repair

A repaired or rerouted line must use pipe approved by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and be pressure tested before it is covered. Water piping must also be listed for drinking water under NSF/ANSI 61.
