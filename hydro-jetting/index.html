Emergency Hydro Jetting in Oakdale, CA

When a line keeps clogging after a cable run, the pipe wall is usually coated, not just blocked. Hydro jetting uses pressurized water to scour that coating away and restore the full diameter of the pipe.

We jet kitchen drains, main lines and commercial lines in Oakdale, and we camera the pipe first so we only jet what can handle it.

When a cable is not enough

A cable punches a hole through a clog, and water drains for a while. The grease or scale on the pipe wall stays behind and starts building again, which is why some lines need service every few months.

A jetter sends water through a hose to a nozzle with rear facing jets. The jets pull the hose forward while spraying the walls, and the debris is flushed down the line toward the main. The result is a pipe that looks close to its original bore.

What hydro jetting removes

Grease

Kitchen and restaurant lines coat with cooled fat that cables only poke through.

Mineral scale

Hard water leaves deposits on cast iron and galvanized lines.

Soap and sludge

Laundry and shower lines build a dense film over years.

Roots

Fine roots that enter at joints are cut and flushed out, though the joint still needs repair.

Sand and silt

Debris that settles in low spots of an older line.

Paper blockages

Packed toilet paper and wipes in a main are cleared and flushed.

Why we camera before we jet

Water pressure is not right for every pipe. A cracked clay line, a collapsed section or a badly offset joint can be damaged or flooded by jetting, so we look first. Our sewer inspection shows what condition the line is in and where problems are.

If the camera finds a break, we recommend sewer line repair before jetting. If the pipe is sound, we choose a nozzle and pressure that suit it and clean the line from the cleanout outward.

Jetting for businesses

Restaurants, shops and offices with floor drains and grease lines benefit most. A regular jetting schedule prevents the sudden backup that closes a kitchen on a busy night. See commercial plumbing for the wider service.

We can set a schedule based on how fast your line fills. A busy kitchen may need quarterly service, while a small office line may need it once a year or only when problems appear.

What to expect on the day

  1. We locate the cleanout and run a camera to check the pipe.
  2. We set up the jetter and protect the area around the access point.
  3. The hose is fed through the line while the nozzle scours the walls.
  4. We run the camera again so you can see the cleaned pipe and any defects.

The visit takes a few hours for most lines. We leave the work area clean and tell you what the camera found.

Why code compliant cleanouts matter for jetting

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), reflected in the California Plumbing Code, requires accessible cleanouts on building drains and sewers. Those fittings are also where a jetter hose enters the line, so a missing cleanout is something we recommend adding.
