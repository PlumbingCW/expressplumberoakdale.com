Soft water in Waterford and why it matters

Waterford takes all of its water from groundwater wells, and its 2021 water quality data listed hardness averaging about 33.5 ppm, with a range from 0 to 45. Sodium averaged about 16.7 ppm and nitrate about 3.23 ppm. That is soft water, far softer than Riverbank or Oakdale.

Soft water leaves little scale, which is good for heaters and fixtures. It can also be more aggressive toward some metals, so pinhole leaks in older copper deserve attention. If you see green stains or small wet spots, leak detection is a sensible first step. Always check the latest city data.

  • Close the main valve if water is spraying or flooding.
  • Switch off the water heater if it is leaking.

Older homes and larger lots

Many Waterford properties sit on generous lots, with trees, outbuildings and long runs of pipe. Roots follow the moisture in a sewer line, and an old clay or cast iron lateral gives them easy entry at every joint.

When drains slow in several fixtures at once, start with sewer line cleaning and a camera look. If the same line clogs again, a repair at the root point is the lasting answer, and our sewer line repair page lists the options.

Preparing for cold nights

Waterford sees a few freezing nights each winter, which is enough to split an exposed pipe. Insulate outdoor lines, disconnect hoses and open the cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during a hard freeze.

If a pipe does freeze, do not use an open flame. Close the main, open the tap to relieve pressure and call us. A slow thaw with a hair dryer is safe for a pipe you can reach, though a split pipe will leak as soon as it thaws.
