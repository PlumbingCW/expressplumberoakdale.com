Downtown Oakdale

Plumber in Downtown Oakdale, CA

Downtown Oakdale is the city's historic core, where F Street meets Sierra Avenue at the old railroad depot and older houses sit a few blocks from shops and restaurants. This page is for people who live or run a business in that part of town.

Where Downtown Oakdale sits

A California State Parks historic resources document puts the greatest concentration of older homes and commercial buildings between Oak Street on the west and Fifth Street on the east. The city treats its central blocks as a design district, with guidelines for windows, doors, roofs, materials, color and signage along F Street and Yosemite Avenue.

A 1986 survey of that core recorded 214 residences, 135 of them Craftsman bungalows, with surveyed properties dating from 1870 to 1940. Oakdale was incorporated in 1906. Only the central part of the area was surveyed, so treat Oak Street to Fifth Street as a rough guide and not as a boundary.

For a homeowner, the useful point is that age varies block by block. A house from the 1920s may have had its pipes replaced once, twice or never, and only an inspection tells you which.

Plumbing questions that suit older streets

These come up on any street with older housing. They are not a claim about your house in particular.

  • Drains that keep slowing down: a sewer camera inspection shows the actual pipe before anyone digs.
  • Supply lines that look mixed or tired: repiping can be planned in phases so the house stays usable.
  • A damp spot or a climbing water bill: leak detection finds the source before walls are opened.
  • Repeat clogs in a kitchen or main line: drain cleaning clears the line, and the camera tells you why it keeps coming back.

Homes and small shops side by side

Downtown blocks mix houses with cafes, shops and restaurants, and the plumbing needs differ. Kitchens that handle grease need hydro jetting more often than a house does, and a shop on the same block may need commercial plumbing help with floor drains and restroom fixtures.

If a job touches the outside of a building in the design district, ask the City of Oakdale at (209) 845-3625 whether any review applies before work starts. The city can also point you to the right office for plumbing permits.

Before you call

  • Close the main shutoff if water is running and you cannot stop it.
  • Note where the problem shows up and when it began.
  • Find the sewer cleanout if you know where it is.
  • Take a photo of the damage or the fixture involved.

A short, clear description lets the plumber ask the right questions and give you an idea of the price before work begins.

Water in Oakdale

The City of Oakdale gets its water from groundwater wells. In the city report we reviewed, total hardness averaged about 88 mg/L, which is moderately hard. That report is from 2013, so ask the city for its latest figures. Moderate hardness leaves mineral film in water heaters and faucets over the years, which is one more reason to check older fixtures.
