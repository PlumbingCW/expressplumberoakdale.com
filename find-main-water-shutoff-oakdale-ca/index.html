How to Find the Main Water Shutoff in Oakdale, CA Homes

The main water shutoff is usually where the water line enters the house, often near the front hose bib, on a garage wall near the water heater or in a utility room. Find it now, test it and tag it, so you can stop a flood in seconds.

This guide shows where to look, how to tell a ball valve from a gate valve and what to do when the valve is stuck or missing.

  • Published November 16, 2026

Where to look first

  1. Walk to the front of the house and look near the outside hose bib on the wall facing the street.
  2. Check the garage wall, especially near the water heater or the entry for the line.
  3. Look in a utility room, a laundry room or a closet on an exterior wall.
  4. Check under the kitchen sink and in a crawlspace if your home has one.
  5. Look in the meter box near the street for the utility valve.

On slab homes the line often rises through the wall on the street side. If you can find the hose bib, the main is usually close by.

Which type of valve do you have

ValveHow it looksHow to close it
Ball valveLever handle, usually straight with the pipe when openTurn the lever a quarter turn across the pipe
Gate valveRound wheel handleTurn clockwise several times until it stops
Meter valveIn the meter box, a flat tab or a stemClose with a meter key or a wrench, if permitted

A ball valve is the one you want. It closes fully with a quarter turn and does not wear the way a gate valve does.

Meters and shutoffs in Oakdale

The City of Oakdale supplies water from its own groundwater wells, and the meter belongs to the system. The valve at the meter box is the utility side of the connection, and the house valve is yours. Use the house valve first, and call the city if it fails and the water is flooding.

Older homes near the original townsite may have old gate valves that have never been closed. A valve that has sat for decades can seize or leak when turned, so test it gently now, not in an emergency.

Test the valve once a year

  1. Open a tap

    Run a cold tap in the house so you can watch the flow.

  2. Close the valve

    Turn it off slowly and confirm the flow stops.

  3. Reopen it

    Open it fully, then back it off a little if it is a gate valve.

  4. Check for drips

    Look around the stem and the packing for any moisture.

If it will not turn or it drips after testing, call us. Forcing a seized valve can make a small problem into a flood.

Mark it and tell the family

Put a bright tag on the valve and tell everyone in the house where it is. In an emergency you may not be home, and a visitor or a family member needs to find it fast.

Keep a small adjustable wrench, a flashlight and a meter key near it. A photo of the valve on your phone helps too, since stress makes people forget.

Other shutoffs worth knowing

Each fixture has its own small valve, called an angle stop or a supply stop. Toilets have one on the wall behind the tank, and sinks have two under the basin. These valves let you stop a single leak without losing water in the whole house.

Water heaters have a cold supply valve above the tank, and washing machines have two valves on the wall box. Learn where each one is and test them once a year with the main.

What to do after you close it

Open the lowest tap in the house to drain pressure from the lines and slow the leak. Turn off the water heater if the lines are empty, since an electric element can burn out without water.

Then call a plumber and describe what you see. If water touched outlets or the panel, keep clear and call an electrician too. Take photos for your insurer before cleanup begins.

Teaching kids and guests

A shutoff only helps if someone can use it. Show teenagers and house sitters where the valve is and which way it turns. A quick walk through takes two minutes and can save a floor.

Renters should ask the landlord for the location and note it in writing. If a valve is blocked by storage, clear the path now, because a blocked valve is as useless as a missing one.

If you cannot find it

Not every house has an obvious valve. If you cannot find one, the water line to the house may only have the meter valve, which is not a good place for a quick shutoff. Installing a house valve is a short plumbing job.

We install full port ball valves at the house and can add a second at the heater. After an emergency, leak detection or burst pipe repair can follow. We serve Riverbank, Escalon and Oakdale.

Shutoff valves and the code

The Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), reflected in the California Plumbing Code, calls for an accessible shutoff on the water service to a building and a valve at the water heater. A full port ball valve is the type most plumbers choose.
