Repiping in Oakdale, CA

Pipes that leak again and again, water that runs brown after sitting and pressure that never recovers are signs the supply system is wearing out. A repipe replaces the failing lines with new pipe so the problem stops.

We plan the work to keep disruption low, with a clear scope and a firm price before we open a wall.

  • Phased and planned

Signs it is time to repipe

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different spots
  • Brown or rusty water after the house has sat
  • Low pressure that gets worse over the years
  • Galvanized steel or early plastic pipe that is known to fail
  • Stains on walls and ceilings that return after repair

One leak is a repair. Several leaks in a few years usually mean the pipe wall is thinning everywhere, and each patch buys only a little time.

Which pipe is in your house

Homes built before the 1960s often have galvanized steel, which rusts inside and closes up. Houses from the 1970s to the 1990s may have copper, which lasts but can pit, or polybutylene, a gray plastic with a poor record. Newer builds use PEX or CPVC.

We identify what you have by looking at the pipe at the meter, the water heater and under sinks. That tells us how likely it is to fail and what the best replacement plan is.

Most whole house repipes in this area use PEX for the speed and flexibility. Copper is a good choice where you want it, and we install either.

How a repipe works in an occupied home

  1. Assess and plan

    We map every fixture and decide on routes through walls, attic or crawlspace.

  2. Open small access points

    Rather than tearing out whole walls, we cut access holes and fish the new lines.

  3. Connect and test

    The new lines are tied in and pressure tested before the old lines are abandoned.

  4. Patch and finish

    Openings are patched ready for paint, and water is restored as soon as the repair allows.

Water is off for part of the work. We schedule around your day and tell you how long to expect, so you can plan meals and showers.

Permits, cost drivers and phasing

A repipe needs a permit and an inspection in California. The City of Oakdale Building Division can confirm the process for your address, and we prepare the work to pass inspection.

Cost depends on the size of the house, the number of fixtures, wall access and finish. If a full repipe is out of reach right now, we can phase it, starting with the worst section, such as the lines that leaked, so you spend where the risk is highest.

What you can expect after the work

New lines usually bring steadier pressure, clearer water and far fewer leak calls. Water that tasted of metal or ran brown after a quiet night often improves once old galvanized lines are gone.

Many insurers look kindly on updated plumbing, and a documented repipe with a passed inspection is a selling point at resale. We provide the permit sign off and a note of the materials used, so you have records for your files.

Approved materials and permits

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), lists the pipe materials approved for drinking water, including copper and PEX, and requires a permit and inspection for a repipe. Materials in contact with drinking water must be listed to NSF/ANSI 61.
